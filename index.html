Abstract

Similar tactile contacts can call for different corrections depending on the configuration of the hand, object and target. Rather than supplying touch and wrist vision separately, fusing them before policy injection better grounds tactile features in visual context, yielding more accurate contact representations. We introduce Wrist-Informed Tactile Representations (WiTac), a pretrained encoder that learns such representations before action generation. Pretrained on nearly 300 hours of SharpaDex data, WiTac deeply fuses tactile signals with hand motion and wrist views into a comprehensive contact representation. Conditioning downstream VLA policies (GR00T N1.7 and π0.5) on WiTac substantially improves task success over vision-only base policies across four contact-rich manipulation tasks (+28.8% and +15.0% on average), and further outperforms WiTac-NW by 18.8% and 7.5%, respectively. Offline analyses further show that incorporating wrist vision yields more accurate contact descriptors, stronger joint task–contact discrimination, greater feature stability under tactile noise, and clearer action-direction readout from similar contacts.

Insight

Wrist context resolves ambiguity in fingertip contact

Touch describes local contact. Wrist vision reveals how the hand, object, and target are arranged. Fusing them before policy injection grounds tactile features in visual context, yielding contact representations with more explicit spatial information.

Why is touch alone ambiguous?

Similar fingertip deformations may occur with different object orientations or target alignments. Local contact information does not fully specify the correction needed to complete the task.

Why fuse before policy injection?

WiTac lets tactile tokens query wrist visual features, so each finger’s contact is represented together with its surrounding spatial context before it reaches the action expert.

Method

Learning wrist-informed contact tokens

WiTac combines tactile histories, hand kinematics, and wrist images into ten finger-level tokens. The encoder is pretrained through complementary self-supervised objectives, then frozen to condition a VLA policy.

(a) Contact encoding in wrist context

An eight-frame tactile history is encoded for each of the ten fingers. Wrist-relative fingertip poses and robot state anchor these tactile features spatially. Finger tokens first interact with one another, then query patch features from both wrist cameras, producing ten finger-level contact tokens.

(b) Pretraining preserves touch and visual context

Five objectives in three groups train the shared representation on nearly 300 hours of SharpaDex data. The DINOv3 visual backbone remains frozen, while the tactile encoders and fusion blocks are optimized jointly.

  • Contact state and history: reconstruct contact descriptors, recent deformation changes, and fingertip-motion histories.
  • Conditional visual recovery: recover masked wrist-image features through the contact-token bottleneck.
  • Physical response: predict future fingertip wrench changes conditioned on an action prefix, only during pretraining.

(c) Gated injection into the action expert

The pretrained WiTac encoder is frozen for policy learning. Selected action blocks attend to the contact tokens through a learned gate. The same interface supports GR00T N1.7 and π0.5, preserving each policy’s native visual, language, and state inputs. Pretraining prediction heads are removed for inference.

Experiments

Contact representations improve real-world manipulation

We evaluate battery insertion, bowl wiping, USB reinsertion, and cabinet opening, with 20 trials per method per task. WiTac improves average success from 37.5% to 66.3% with GR00T N1.7, and from 43.8% to 58.8% with π0.5.

Representation Analysis

What does wrist context add to touch?

01

More accurate contact reconstruction

Across 9,632 validation windows from 602 episodes, WiTac reconstructs contact area, intensity, and center more accurately than WiTac-NW. Wrist context helps preserve the geometry and strength of local touch.

7.57% · 10.00% · 2.64% lower area, intensity, and center errors, respectively, across all validation windows.

02

Joint readout of task context and contact state

Frozen WiTac features distinguish both the task and its contact state. Across 12- and 20-class evaluations on two test splits, WiTac improves macro-F1 over wrist-only DINO and WiTac-NW. It is comparable to simple feature concatenation in three settings, with a larger gain on the 20-class confirmation split.

03

More stable features under tactile noise

With Gaussian noise added to tactile inputs (σ = 3 on the 8-bit intensity scale), WiTac reduces feature drift across all four tasks. GR00T N1.7 action error also falls by 7.35–22.76%; action-level gains for π0.5 vary by task.

35.6–44.1% lower feature drift relative to WiTac-NW, with wrist images and robot state held unchanged.

04

Clearer action-direction readout from similar contacts

Similar tactile contacts can precede different corrective motions. On 25 contact-matched test pairs per task, WiTac achieves the strongest mean directional alignment across all four tasks using equal-capacity linear probes on frozen features.
